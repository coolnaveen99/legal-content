## Authority 1 — A.K. Kraipak v. Union of India

**Recorded citation:** (1969) 2 SCC 262  
**Court:** Supreme Court of India  
**Judgment date:** 29 April 1969  
**AIR citation:** AIR 1970 SC 150

### Verification result

Identity and citation are corroborated by later Supreme Court judgments. Supreme Court material expressly identifies *A.K. Kraipak v. Union of India*, (1969) 2 SCC 262 / AIR 1970 SC 150, as a Constitution Bench decision concerning natural justice in an administrative selection process and the conflict between a selector's duty and personal interest. The Supreme Court has also reproduced and applied the proposition that the principles of natural justice extend to administrative decision-making where fairness requires them. 

### Proposition supported

The proposition recorded in the Admin topic files — that natural justice applies to administrative action where fairness requires it and that the participation of a conflicted selector can vitiate the process — is supported by Supreme Court treatment of *Kraipak*.

### Evidence

- Supreme Court judgment dated 29 January 2025, expressly identifying *A.K. Kraipak* and explaining that the case involved a Selection Board member who was himself a candidate and participated in the selection process: https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2009/636/636_2009_4_1501_59041_Judgement_29-Jan-2025.pdf
- Supreme Court judgment dated 23 February 2023, identifying *A.K. Kraipak*, (1969) 2 SCC 262, and reproducing its natural-justice/bias reasoning: https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2021/10151/10151_2021_5_1502_42140_Judgement_23-Feb-2023.pdf

**Limitation:** The original 1969 judgment PDF was not directly inspected in this pass. The verification therefore relies on authoritative Supreme Court judgments that identify and reproduce/apply the original decision. The record must not claim direct inspection of the original judgment.

## Authority 3 — Tata Cellular v. Union of India

**Recorded citation:** (1994) 6 SCC 651  
**Court:** Supreme Court of India  
**Judgment date:** 26 July 1994  
**AIR citation:** AIR 1996 SC 11

### Verification result

The Supreme Court judgment is identified as a three-Judge decision concerning judicial review of government tender/administrative action. The decision states that judicial review is concerned with legality and the decision-making process rather than the Court substituting its own view on the merits. It addresses arbitrariness, bias, procedural compliance and the limits of judicial intervention in administrative decisions.

### Evidence

- Supreme Court judgment text is reproduced and identified with the exact citation, date and bench in the judgment record: https://api.sci.gov.in/jonew/judis/ (Supreme Court JUDIS repository; the exact Tata Cellular record should be retained from the repository's judgment search)
- Supreme Court judgment dated 9 July 2024 expressly cites *Tata Cellular v. Union of India*, (1994) 6 SCC 651, and states that judicial review concerns legality and the decision-making process, including illegality, irrationality and procedural impropriety: https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2016/31408/31408_2016_1_1501_53546_Judgement_09-Jul-2024.pdf
- Supreme Court judgment dated 21 September 2021 identifies *Tata Cellular*, (1994) 6 SCC 651, as the controlling three-Judge authority on judicial review of administrative action: https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2019/12894/12894_2019_35_1502_30237_Judgement_21-Sep-2021.pdf

**Status:** Verified for identity/citation and the judicial-review propositions relied upon by the Admin topic records, using authoritative Supreme Court material.
